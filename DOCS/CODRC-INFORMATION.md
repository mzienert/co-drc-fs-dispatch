# Durango Interagency Dispatch Center (CODRC) - Comprehensive Information

## Overview

The Durango Interagency Dispatch Center (CODRC) serves as the critical coordination hub for wildfire and emergency response across 4.5 million acres of Southwestern Colorado. Located in the Durango Tech Center, the dispatch center extends its coverage from the Utah state line in the west to the Continental Divide in the east, and from the New Mexico state line in the south to the Montrose Dispatch Center boundary in the north. The service area encompasses diverse terrain with elevations ranging from 5,000 feet to over 14,000 feet, including the major communities of Cortez, Durango, and Pagosa Springs.

## Interagency Partnership

The Durango Dispatch Center serves as the interagency focal point for resource mobilization across five federal agencies: the San Juan National Forest (U.S. Forest Service), Tres Rios Field Office (Bureau of Land Management), Southern Ute Agency (Bureau of Indian Affairs), Ute Mountain Ute Agency (Bureau of Indian Affairs), and Mesa Verde National Park (National Park Service). These agencies share the cost of dispatch services, creating a unified emergency response system across jurisdictional boundaries.

## Operations & Capabilities

The center operates with a core team of six staff members, mostly former wildland firefighters, who work 10-hour shifts that can extend to 16 hours during emergencies. Dispatchers coordinate a comprehensive array of firefighting resources including aircraft, helicopters, fire engines, and bulldozers, while managing detailed logistics from tracking lightning strikes to arranging supplies for field crews. Using advanced mapping programs with high-level capabilities to track heat, smoke, and lightning strikes, the dispatch center handled 49 wildfires and 336 other incidents in a recent fire season. During major emergencies like the 2018 416 Fire—which burned 54,120 acres and required over 1,000 personnel—the center scaled up operations by adding eight extra dispatchers to coordinate the massive response effort.

## Local Fire Resources

The Durango area hosts world-class fire management resources as part of the San Juan National Forest Fire Program, including the San Juan Interagency Hotshots (a 25-person Type 1 crew), the Durango Helitack Crew (a 15-person nationally deployable helicopter crew), and the Durango Airtanker Base—the highest elevation tanker base in the nation at 6,685 feet. These resources, co-located at the Durango/La Plata County Airport approximately 15 miles south of Durango, provide rapid initial attack capabilities for the region while also deploying nationwide during major wildfire incidents.

## Staffing & Operations

### Core Team
- **Staff Size:** 6 dispatchers (core team)
- **Background:** Mostly former wildland firefighters
- **Shift Schedule:** 10-hour shifts (extendable to 16 hours during emergencies)
- **Emergency Scaling:** Can add additional dispatchers during major incidents (e.g., 8 extra during 416 Fire)

### Responsibilities
- Track lightning strikes
- Coordinate firefighting resources (aircraft, engines, bulldozers)
- Manage detailed logistics and supply coordination
- Monitor air resources
- Use advanced mapping and tracking technology
- Coordinate with multiple federal and tribal agencies

### Technology & Tools
- Advanced mapping programs with high-level capabilities
- Heat and smoke tracking systems
- Lightning strike monitoring
- Air resource management systems
- Unmanned Aerial System (Drone) program

## Fire Management Resources

### San Juan Interagency Hotshots
- **Type:** Type 1 Interagency Hotshot Crew
- **Crew Size:** 25 personnel
- **Base Location:** Durango, Colorado
- **Season:** Available early April to mid-October
- **Capability:** National deployment

### Durango Helitack Crew
- **Crew Size:** 15 personnel
- **Type:** Forest Service Exclusive Use Helitack Crew
- **Status:** National Shared Resource
- **Training:** Tactical and logistical use of helicopters for fire management
- **Deployment:** Available nationwide

### Durango Airtanker Base
- **Location:** Durango/La Plata County Airport (15 miles south of Durango)
- **Elevation:** 6,685 feet (highest elevation tanker base in the nation)
- **Co-location:** Shares facility with Durango Helitack Crew

### Additional Resources
- Type 1 Wildland Fire Module
- Unmanned Aerial System (Drone) program
- Fire Prevention/Mitigation/Education Program
- Engines, Suppression Modules, and Fuels Crews on 3 Ranger Districts

## Interagency Coordination

The Durango Dispatch Center operates as part of the broader Rocky Mountain Coordinating Group, which includes:
- Bureau of Indian Affairs
- Bureau of Land Management
- National Park Service
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service
- U.S. Forest Service
- Colorado and Kansas state agencies
- Rocky Mountain Fire Executive Council

The center functions as a "symphony conductor," unifying different agency operations and ensuring seamless coordination across federal, tribal, and state jurisdictions.
